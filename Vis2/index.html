Recurrent heatwaves also appear frequently, reflecting Australia’s vulnerability to extreme weather. Other major tragedies include the Cataraqui shipwreck, Cyclone Mahina, and the sinking of the AHS Centaur. Together, these events illustrate the diverse and devastating nature of disasters that have shaped Australia’s history and its approach to emergency resilience.

What Disasters Are Happening?

Interactivity within the line chart

The most common disasters in Australia are classified as Extreme Weather events, encompassing floods, storms, cyclones, maritime or coastal incidents, and tornadoes. Floods regularly affect inland and coastal regions, causing widespread damage and displacement. Cyclones and severe storms frequently impact northern and eastern Australia, bringing destructive winds, heavy rain, and storm surges. Maritime and coastal disasters, including shipwrecks and erosion, have also left a lasting mark on Australia’s history. Although less frequent, tornadoes occasionally occur in parts of Queensland and New South Wales. Overall, these events highlight Australia’s exposure to diverse and often severe weather conditions.
